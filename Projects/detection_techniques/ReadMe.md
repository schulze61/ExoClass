# Measuring Planet Mass, Radius, and Density

Project description:
You have been assigned an exoplanet: GJ 1214 b or HD 189733 b. Your job is to estimate the mass, radius, and density of the planet using radial velocity and transit light curve data and place these values in a broader exoplanet context. 

You will need to:
1.	Measure the planet mass and uncertainty based on the radial velocity data. 
2.	Measure the planet radius and uncertainty based on the transit data. 
3.	Calculate the planet density and uncertainty based on the mass and radius measurements. 

The project outline is described in detail the corresponding jupyter notebook, but the main questions are:
1.	What is the planet mass and the uncertainty?
2.	What is the planet radius and the uncertainty?
3.	What is the planet density and the uncertainty?
4.	How do your estimates compare to the literature?

Goals:
1.	 Be able to use NEA and other tools to look up individual exoplanet data and references.
2.	Strengthen the understanding of measuring planet mass and radius based on observables. 
3.	Be able to fit data from exoplanet observations.
4.	Understand the measurement uncertainty in observations and how it propagates to measured physical quantities. 
5.	Understand how density of a planet is measured and how a single data point is placed in the context of a population. 

Deliverables:
1. A 10-min oral presentation + up to 5 mins for questions
	-	The presentation should cover motivation, methods, results, and conclusions. 
	-	The presentation should address all questions in the project
	-	The presenter should acknowledge the contribution of each team member in the form of an acknowledgement slide (just put this at the end and leave it up at the end of the presentation – no need to spend time reading it out loud)

2. A written report. The report should be less than 5-page and cover motivation, methods, results, conclusions, and contribution statement for each team member. References and acknowledgements do not count towards the 5-page limit.
	-	Abstract (no more than 300 words) – needs to include the main results
	-	Introduction -- ~1 paragraphs outlining the broader context of the project and ~1 paragraph broadly motivating the project and the planet you are looking at
	-	Methods – clearly outline the techniques employed, assumptions made, calculations and main equations.
	-	Results – this section should answer all questions. Figures and tables should be easy to read and understand. All figures and tables should be numbered and reference in the text
	-	Discussion/conclusion – circle back to the broader context and discuss in detail where your planet fits into it based on your results.
	-	Acknowledgments -- the report should acknowledge the contribution of each team member when appropriate, e.g., specifying leader and contributors for each section of the report.
